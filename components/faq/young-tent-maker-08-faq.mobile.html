Frequently Asked Questions

Questions About Being a YTM?

Who can become a Young Tent-Maker?

Young adults under 35 years old who have graduated from Vision School and have served for six months in InterCP International Young Adult ministry can be commissioned.

How long is the commission?

YTMs serve one year on the field in a team, after one month of training prior to dispatch at InterCP International Headquarters.

When are YTMs sent out to the field?

There are two dispatches each year: the summer dispatch in June and July, and the winter dispatch in December and January, each after a month of training.

How do I learn more?

Email northamerica@intercp.org and ask about the next Young Tent-Maker dispatch.

What do Young Tent-Makers do in the frontier nations?

YTMs use their giftings, work experience, and specialties to pioneer church planting movements. On the field, they:  

  1. Go in teams to the last remaining frontiers to plant new churches.
  2. Make disciples of all nations through incarnation mission as a team.
  3. Are trained to become leaders of the global mission movement.  

Upon their return, YTMs keep applying their training by leading campus and workplace ministries at home.

Can I serve longer than one year?

Yes. Young adults may also go as 2-year YG Missionaries or 2–5 year missionaries.

Is a YTM different from SM?

SMs are current undergraduate college students over 18; YTMs are young adults under 35. Both go in teams for one year.

What training is needed before going out to the field?

Vision School, six months of ministry, and a month of training.

Who do I contact with questions?

For more about Young Tent Makers, please reach InterCP International North America by email or through the contact page.

What legacy do Young Tent-Makers carry forward into the nations?

Since the 1806 Haystack Prayer Movement, God has used the young generation for world evangelization. From the 1886 Mount Hermon Conference came the Student Volunteer Movement: 100,000 young people committed their lives to the Great Commission, and 20,500 set sail for nations that had never heard the gospel.
