# Image sourcing and representation notes

**Status:** Working guidance for concept review
**Primary market:** United States
**Last updated:** August 26, 2026

## Goal

Show Vision School as an authentic, multiethnic, multigenerational Christian learning community in the United States while retaining an honest connection to its Korean and global origins.

Representation should be visible across the full page, not forced into a single image. The overall image set should naturally include Black, Latino, Asian, white, African, and other diaspora participants in activities that match the surrounding content.

## Selection rules

- Prefer real Vision School, worship, lecture, prayer, small-group, fellowship, and community scenes.
- Match the photograph to the claim. A travel or mission-field image must not be presented as a U.S. classroom.
- Favor candid interaction over posed diversity.
- Use Korean participants where the image communicates the program’s origin, a real Vision School activity, or its global character.
- Avoid tokenism, demographic labeling in alt text, identifiable minors without confirmed releases, and images that disclose sensitive field locations or participants.
- Before production, confirm ownership, model/participant releases, regional publishing permission, source credit requirements, and a suitable high-resolution original.

## Reviewed but not used in this preview

- Vision School display-wall image: relevant to the program, but it shows participants mainly from behind and is not strong enough for a primary people-centered moment.
- All Nations Adults desert group: warm and multigenerational, but it reads as travel rather than a U.S. Vision School experience.
- Youth walking through a historic city: mission-context imagery, but not clearly connected to the course experience.
- Field/community landscape images: potentially useful for a future global-story or history page, but too ambiguous for the current U.S. class preview.

## Production follow-up

Build a reviewed media library with source URL, photographer/owner, consent status, represented activity, approximate date and location, allowed regions/channels, crop restrictions, and approved alt text for every published asset.

For video-derived stills, also retain the official video title, channel, source timestamp, and whether the visible event watermark must remain in frame. Keep the blue color veil in CSS so the source image remains intact and the treatment can be adjusted without regenerating the asset.
